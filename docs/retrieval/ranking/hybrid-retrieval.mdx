Each temporal memory graph indexes every fact's text in a full-text search index and, when an embedding is supplied with it, in a vector index. Its edges link each fact to the topics and entities it is filed under.

<Note title="Seeds, not answers" type="note">

**The indexes are seeds, not answers**, and a retrieval score is **the seeds' own mass plus transmitted surplus**.

</Note>

## The seeds

A recall pulls candidates from each source it can use, each carrying its raw retrieval mass; the text and vector indexes are each asked for `seed-size` of them, or `top:` when that is larger:

| Source | Mass it carries |
|---|---|
| text index | BM25 $\times$ coverage: the share of the query's idf mass the fact matched, so a fact matching every query term keeps its full BM25 score and a partial match is discounted by the informativeness of the terms it missed |
| vector index | $1 / (1 + \text{distance})$ similarity |
| named anchors, on a recall with no term or vector | a unit mass for each named anchor the fact is filed under |

A fact found by more than one source carries the sum of their masses.

### What the text index matches

A fact is indexed under its terms and a recall's keywords are read the same way, so the two line up: text is case-folded, English stop words are dropped, and each remaining term is reduced to its stem, which is how `running` finds a fact written with `runs`. The stop words are [Snowball's English list](https://snowballstem.org/algorithms/english/stop.txt), the one written for the stemmer: function words only (pronouns, articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, conjunctions), so every content word stays searchable. Negations are not stop words: `not`, `no` and `nor` are indexed, so a fact that something is not so is told apart from one that it is. A term is a maximal run of letters, digits and symbols in Unicode category So (Symbol, other), the class that holds emoji, check marks and marks such as `©`. A fact written as `🍊 🍋 🍌` is therefore found by `recall '🍊'` (quoted, since an emoji is not a letter to the query grammar). Every other character delimits: whitespace, punctuation, and the math, currency and modifier symbols, so `c++` indexes as `c` and `$5` as `5`. Joiners and variation selectors delimit too, so a multi-character emoji sequence indexes as each of its symbols.

## The transmission

When a recall with a term or vector also names a `topic:` or `entity:` anchor and takes `depth:1` or `depth:2` (see [Retrieval lanes](/docs/retrieval/ranking/depth)), that mass then flows into the fact–anchor graph. Every anchor the query touches, meaning every topic or entity a seed is filed under, observes the total seed mass on its members and compares it with its fair share: its degree times the **background rate** $\rho_0$, the query's seed mass per unit of degree across the anchors it reached.

Only the excess above background is transmitted, and it is transmitted **once, not once per member**:

- the surplus is divided across the anchor's edges, each member receiving its per-edge share;
- attenuated per edge ($\alpha = 0.5$, $\alpha^2$ over the two-edge seed → anchor → fact path);
- with the member's own mass excluded, so a fact never funds its own boost.

A fact's relevance is its own mass plus everything transmitted to it.

## One sentence of intuition

> *An anchor may only speak when its members matched better than its size alone predicted — hubs are heard exactly when they are surprising, silent when they are merely large, and never louder than their surplus divided among everyone listening.*

### The window

Facts written one after the other under the same anchor are usually one exchange, and scored one by one each is a fragment of it. With [`db.window-gamma`](/docs/configuration/reference/database#window) set, a fact borrows that share of each temporal neighbour's term counts and length when the text index scores it, so a question holding half the query and its answer holding the other half both cover the query, and a fact beside a match becomes a candidate even when it holds no query term itself. Neighbours are the facts written just before and after under a shared anchor. It is on by default; a negative share turns it off.

## Where the list stops

A recall returns its `top:` best candidates — and, with the score cutoff on, fewer: `db.min-score-ratio` drops the hits whose relevance, before recency decay and the ranking boost, is below a fraction of the best hit's. The scores and their order are unchanged, so turning it on only shortens the list. It is off by default; see [Database settings](/docs/configuration/reference/database#score-cutoff).

With [`db.aggregate`](/docs/configuration/reference/database#dbaggregate) on, a recall whose matches spread across many sessions at similar scores, the shape of an aggregation question, folds the candidates that matched by the same rare term into one group hit holding them all, best first, and spends the freed slots on the next best facts. The gate is the ranking's spread, so a specific question is returned as facts. It is on by default; `name = "none"` turns it off.

## Why a recall needs a seed

Without a seed there is no mass to transmit. That is why a recall must carry at least one term, anchor or vector, and why `recall top:3` is an error rather than an unfiltered dump.
