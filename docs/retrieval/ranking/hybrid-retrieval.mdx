Each temporal memory graph indexes every fact's text in a full-text search index and, when an embedding is supplied with it, in a vector index. Its edges link each fact to the topics and entities it is filed under.

<Note title="Seeds, not answers" type="note">

**The indexes are seeds, not answers**, and a retrieval score is **what the text, the vector and the graph each say about a fact**, measured against everything the recall could return.

</Note>

## The seeds

A recall pulls candidates from each source it can use, each carrying its raw retrieval mass; the text and vector indexes are each asked for `seed-size` of them, or `top:` when that is larger:

| Source | Mass it carries |
|---|---|
| text index | BM25 $\times$ coverage: the share of the query's idf mass the fact matched, so a fact matching every query term keeps its full BM25 score and a partial match is discounted by the informativeness of the terms it missed |
| vector index | $1 / (1 + \text{distance})$ similarity, which counts for more the more of the question the best text hit missed ([vector weight](/docs/configuration/reference/database#vector-weight)) |
| named anchors, on a recall with no term or vector | a unit mass for each named anchor the fact is filed under |

A fact found by more than one source carries the sum of their masses, the vector's weighted, as its **seed mass** $m$: what it puts into the graph.

### What the text index matches

A fact is indexed under its terms and a recall's keywords are read the same way, so the two line up: text is case-folded, English stop words are dropped, and each remaining term is reduced to its stem, which is how `running` finds a fact written with `runs`. The stop words are [Snowball's English list](https://snowballstem.org/algorithms/english/stop.txt), the one written for the stemmer: function words only (pronouns, articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, conjunctions), so every content word stays searchable. Negations are not stop words: `not`, `no` and `nor` are indexed, so a fact that something is not so is told apart from one that it is. A term is a maximal run of letters, digits and symbols in Unicode category So (Symbol, other), the class that holds emoji, check marks and marks such as `©`. A fact written as `🍊 🍋 🍌` is therefore found by `recall '🍊'` (quoted, since an emoji is not a letter to the query grammar). Every other character delimits: whitespace, punctuation, and the math, currency and modifier symbols, so `c++` indexes as `c` and `$5` as `5`. Joiners and variation selectors delimit too, so a multi-character emoji sequence indexes as each of its symbols.

## Named anchors

A `topic:` or `entity:` named beside a term or a vector **adds relevance and removes nothing**. The recall's matches filed under the anchor put their seed mass $M_N$ on it, and each fact filed under it receives that mass divided across the anchor's $d_N$ edges, its own mass excluded:

$$
\frac{M_N - m}{d_N}
$$

It owes no fair share, since the recall asked for that anchor, and an anchor none of the recall's matches is filed under adds nothing. A fact the words never matched but filed under a named anchor is therefore a candidate, and a fact the words matched but filed elsewhere stays one: only `since:` and `until:` narrow what a recall can return. A named anchor is graph evidence, so it needs the graph channel (any `search-algorithm` but `none`), and it acts in every lane, `depth:0` included.

## The transmission

When a recall with a term or vector also names a `topic:` or `entity:` anchor and takes `depth:1` or `depth:2` (see [Retrieval lanes](/docs/retrieval/ranking/depth)), the seed mass also flows through the rest of the fact–anchor graph. Every anchor the query touches, meaning every topic or entity a seed is filed under, observes the total seed mass on its members and compares it with its fair share: its degree times the **background rate** $\rho_0$, the query's seed mass per unit of degree across the anchors it reached.

Only the excess above background is transmitted, and it is transmitted **once, not once per member**:

- the surplus is divided across the anchor's edges, each member receiving its per-edge share;
- with the member's own mass excluded, so a fact never funds its own boost.

The named anchors are observed with the others and count in $\rho_0$, but pass their mass on only through the named share above, never a second time through the round. What a fact receives over every anchor it is filed under is its **graph evidence** $g$.

## The fold

A candidate ends with three channels: its text mass $t$, its vector similarity above the reading's floor $v$ (the lowest similarity among the recall's vector hits, so the farthest of them adds nothing), and its graph evidence $g$. `scoring-algorithm` picks how they fold into one score ([Algorithm settings](/docs/configuration/reference/algorithms)).

**`information`, the default.** The recall's pool is every candidate it could return: the $n$ facts inside its time range. A fact's share $p$ of a channel's total, set against the even share $1/n$, is the ratio $r = n\,p$, and the fact scores the bits each channel gives it, weighted by what that channel tells about the pool:

$$
\text{score} = \sum_{c \in \{t,\,v,\,g\}} w_c \log_2(1 + r_c), \qquad w_c = \frac{I_c}{I_t + I_v + I_g}, \qquad I_c = \sum_{\text{pool}} p_c \log_2(n\,p_c)
$$

$I_c$ is $\log_2 n$ minus the entropy of the channel's shares: a channel that scores every fact alike tells them apart not at all and gets no weight, one concentrated on a few facts gets more, and when no channel tells them apart each counts once. The vector's $I_v$ is multiplied by the [vector weight](/docs/configuration/reference/database#vector-weight), so the embedding leads where the words missed the question. Each channel is measured against its own total, so none needs calibrating against another, and the fold carries no constant. A recall seeded by anchors alone has no channel to measure: each fact scores one unit per named anchor it is filed under.

**`excess`.** A fact scores its seed mass plus its graph evidence, attenuated per edge ($\alpha = 0.5$, $\alpha^2$ over the two-edge seed → anchor → fact path): $m + \alpha^2 g$, its vector similarity counted in $m$ by the vector weight. Graph evidence then only ever adds, which is the [BM25 floor](/docs/retrieval/ranking/guarantees#bm25-floor). The information fold weighs the graph against the text instead, so graph evidence concentrated on a few facts can rank them above a fact the words matched better.

## One sentence of intuition

> *An anchor may only speak when its members matched better than its size alone predicted — hubs are heard exactly when they are surprising, silent when they are merely large, and never louder than their surplus divided among everyone listening.*

An anchor the recall names is the one exception, by design: the question asked for it.

## Where the list stops

A recall returns its `top:` best candidates, or every match when there are fewer.

The slots are spread across the facets the ranking spans, the sessions of a conversation, say: each facet's best fact before any facet's second, the deferred facts after, so ten slots cover as many sessions as the pool offers before deepening any, and the best hit stays first. See [`db.aggregate`](/docs/configuration/reference/database#dbaggregate).

## Why a recall needs a seed

Without a seed there is no mass to transmit. That is why a recall must carry at least one term, anchor or vector, and why `recall top:3` is an error rather than an unfiltered dump.
