## The background rate is the query's own

$\rho_0$ is measured over the anchors a query reaches. The numerator is the total seed mass the query put onto those anchors; the denominator is their **total degree**. Every touched anchor counts — including one that ends up silent, since its mass and size are part of what the query observed — and an anchor the query never reached counts not at all.

Writing $M_A$ for an anchor's observed mass and $d_A$ for its degree, over the touched set $T$:

$$
\rho_0 = \frac{\sum_{A \in T} M_A}{\sum_{A \in T} d_A}
$$

$d_A \, \rho_0$ is then the anchor's **fair share**: the mass it would hold if the query's evidence were spread across the anchors it reached in proportion to their size. An anchor transmits only what it holds above that share.

## A worked example

Three topics: `cluster` files 3 facts, two of which match the query with mass 8 and 6; `hub` files 6 facts, one of which matches with mass 2; `giant` files 10 facts, none of which match, and the query never reaches it.

| Anchor | Degree | Mass | Fair share at $\rho_0 = 16/9$ | Transmits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cluster` | 3 | 14 | 5.33 | yes — it holds well over its share |
| `hub` | 6 | 2 | 10.67 | no — one weak match among six facts |
| `giant` | 10 | — | not touched | no, and it does not weigh in $\rho_0$ |

$\rho_0 = (14 + 2)/(3 + 6) = 16/9$. The third `cluster` fact, which matched nothing, receives $(14 - 0 - 5.33)/3 \approx 2.89$ of graph evidence: the surplus shared across `cluster`'s three edges. The two facts that matched receive smaller shares, $0.22$ and $0.89$, since a fact never funds its own boost. How the evidence enters a score is the fold's: `excess` adds it to the fact's own mass, $2.89$ for the third fact; `information` weighs it against the text (see [The fold](/docs/retrieval/ranking/hybrid-retrieval#the-fold)).

## Why untouched anchors stay out

Counting `giant` would lower $\rho_0$ to $16/19$, and every further unrelated anchor would lower it again. Add enough memory the query has nothing to do with and `hub` — one weak match among six facts — clears its share and starts transmitting, although nothing about this query or the facts it matched has changed. An anchor untouched by the query carries no evidence about it; letting it into the rate would make the same question rank differently depending on how much unrelated memory the graph happens to hold.

Measured over the anchors reached, the bar depends only on what the query observed: its seeds, and the anchors those seeds are filed under.

## What a relative bar implies

The fair shares of the touched anchors add up to exactly the mass they hold:

$$
\sum_{A \in T} \bigl( M_A - d_A \, \rho_0 \bigr) = \sum M - \rho_0 \sum d = 0
$$

so admission is a comparison among the anchors one query reached: an anchor speaks when it concentrates more of the query's evidence than its size accounts for next to the others. Three consequences follow, all of them intended:

- **A query that reaches one anchor it did not name transmits nothing.** That anchor holds exactly its share — $M = d \cdot (M/d)$ — and with nothing to compare it against there is no evidence that it concentrates anything.
- **A query with one seed admits on degree alone.** Every anchor it touches observes the same mass $m$, so an anchor clears its share exactly when its degree is below the touched mean. That is the correct inference: an anchor of degree 3 holding $m$ *is* more surprising than an anchor of degree 300 holding the same $m$.
- **Silence is relative, not absolute.** An anchor falls silent when it holds no more of the query's evidence than its size accounts for next to the other anchors reached, whatever that evidence amounts to in raw units.

`depth:1` raises the admission bar to twice the fair share; `depth:2` admits at the fair share itself. The rate and the fold are the same in both — see [Retrieval lanes](/docs/retrieval/ranking/depth).

## Named anchors

The bar is a null model for the anchors a query discovers: an anchor the seeds happen to reach must show that it concentrates more of the evidence than chance would put there. An anchor the recall names needs no such test, since naming it is the query's own claim that it bears on the question. It transmits whenever a seed reaches it, at either lane, and owes each member only that member's own mass:

$$
\frac{\max\bigl(0,\; M_A - m\bigr)}{d_A}
$$

Naming `hub` in the example above lets it speak. Its five unmatched facts each receive $2/6 \approx 0.33$, and its one match receives nothing, since a fact never funds its own boost. The named anchor weighs in $\rho_0$ like any anchor the query reached. Naming it also seeds the matches filed under it, and those seeds weigh in $\rho_0$ like any others, so the discovered anchors are held to the bar the anchored recall's own evidence sets.

A named anchor filters nothing. A match filed under none of the named anchors keeps its own mass and ranks among the rest; a match filed under one is lifted by what its siblings there matched.

## Choosing the traversal

`search-algorithm` selects the traversal moving seed evidence through the graph:

| Value | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `excess` (default) | the shipped excess-transmission strategy |
| `bfs` | tree-shaped incidence, kept for comparison runs |
| `none` | the graph channel off — text/vector search only |

`scoring-algorithm` selects the fold deriving relevance from the pooled evidence. `information`, the default, measures the text, vector and graph channels against the recall's pool and lets the text and the vector corroborate each other; `excess` adds the surplus to the seed mass, and alone keeps the [BM25 floor](/docs/retrieval/ranking/guarantees#bm25-floor). `rrf` remains available for comparison runs, with its dampening constant fixed at the literature-standard 60, since a comparison baseline is not tunable; it carries no null model. See [Algorithm settings](/docs/configuration/reference/algorithms).
