The same request body and pipeline as [`/q`](/docs/http-api/endpoints/query), **for recalls only**: each hit also carries `contributions`, the per-source sightings its score was folded from, and the result carries the query's `background` rate, omitted when it is zero.

Use it to see *why* a fact ranked where it did; use `/q` when the ranking is all you need — the breakdown costs response tokens, which is why it lives on its own endpoint instead of every recall.

## A contribution

One contribution records one sighting of the hit by one retrieval source:

| Field | Meaning |
|----------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `source` | which stage saw it: `text`, `vector`, `graph` or `anchor` |
| `score` | the source's raw mass: BM25 match mass for `text`, similarity as measured for `vector`, the funding anchor's full observed mass for `graph`, a unit mass for `anchor` |
| `rank` | the hit's position in that source's own result list, 0 first |
| `via` | the anchor a `graph` or `anchor` sighting came through |
| `degree` | that anchor's degree — how many edges its mass is shared across |
| `count` | how many seeds funded the sighting |
| `named` | `true` on a `graph` sighting through an anchor the recall named; omitted otherwise |

An `anchor` sighting belongs to a recall seeded by its anchors alone: the fact was reached because it is filed under an anchor the query named.

<Note title="Contributions are not a sum" type="warning">

`score` on the hit remains the final value — after the fold and recency decay — so **the contributions explain its ingredients rather than summing to it.** The default fold measures each hit against the recall's whole pool, so the contributions explain a score exactly only when the recall returns every candidate, and a recall with a vector also needs the [vector weight](/docs/configuration/reference/database#vector-weight), which the payload does not carry.

</Note>

## Writes are rejected

A `remember` on this endpoint is rejected with 400: a write has no ranking to explain, and explaining must never mutate a graph.

## Reading a breakdown

A `text` sighting is the fact's own match plus what it borrows from its temporal neighbours under the [window](/docs/configuration/reference/database#window), so one can appear on a fact that holds none of the query's words. A `vector` sighting is the fact's own similarity. Together they are its mass $m$. A `graph` sighting is an anchor the fact is filed under, carrying the mass $M_A$ the query's seeds put on it; it adds to the hit only the part of that mass above what the anchor's degree predicts at the `background` rate $\rho_0$, with the fact's own mass excluded, shared across the anchor's edges:

$$
\frac{\max(0,\; M_A - m - d_A \, \rho_0)}{d_A}
$$

A sighting marked `named` comes through an anchor the recall named, which owes no fair share: drop the $d_A \, \rho_0$ term, and it adds $\max(0,\; M_A - m)/d_A$. Summed over a hit's graph sightings, these are its **graph evidence** $g$.

A graph sighting at or below background adds nothing, so a hit can list anchors that did not move its score. A hit with only `graph` contributions matched nothing itself and was preempted in on transmitted surplus alone, which the model permits only when the surplus is above background or the anchor was named.

Under the `information` fold, the default, a hit's channels are its text mass, its vector similarity above the lowest one among the recall's vector hits, and $g$; each is shared out over the recall's $n$ hits, and the hit scores $\sum_c w_c \log_2(1 + n\,p_c)$, where $p_c$ is its share of channel $c$ and $w_c$ the channel's share of the information the channels carry about the pool (see [The fold](/docs/retrieval/ranking/hybrid-retrieval#the-fold)). In the example, the three `polly` facts put $1.4572 + 0.8757 + 0.1145 = 2.4474$ on `polly`, the third through what it borrows from its neighbours under the window, so they get $g = 0.3301$, $0.5239$ and $0.7776$: each the mass of the other two, shared over the anchor's three edges. Over the four hits the text carries $0.42$ bits about the pool and the graph $0.50$, weights $0.46$ and $0.54$, and the first fact scores $0.46 \log_2(1 + 4 \times 0.522) + 0.54 \log_2(1 + 4 \times 0.202) \approx 1.2096$.

Under the `excess` fold the hit scores $m + g$: graph evidence added to the fact's own mass. The fold and the lanes are introduced in [Hybrid retrieval](/docs/retrieval/ranking/hybrid-retrieval).
