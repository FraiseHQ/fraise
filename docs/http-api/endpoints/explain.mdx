The same request body and pipeline as [`/q`](/docs/http-api/endpoints/query), **for recalls only**: each hit also carries `contributions`, the per-source sightings its score was folded from, and the result carries the query's `background` rate, omitted when it is zero.

Use it to see *why* a fact ranked where it did; use `/q` when the ranking is all you need — the breakdown costs response tokens, which is why it lives on its own endpoint instead of every recall.

That is `recall parrot kitchen entity:polly` over four facts, three of them filed under `polly`, in the default lane and fold.

## A contribution

One contribution records one sighting of the hit by one retrieval source:

| Field | Meaning |
|----------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `source` | which stage saw it: `text`, `vector`, `graph`, `anchor` or `named` |
| `score` | the source's raw observation: BM25 match mass for `text`, similarity as measured for `vector`, the funding anchor's full observed mass for `graph`, a unit mass for `anchor`, the seed mass the recall's matches put on a named anchor for `named` |
| `rank` | the hit's position in that source's own result list, 0 first |
| `via` | the anchor a `graph`, `anchor` or `named` sighting came through |
| `degree` | that anchor's degree — how many edges its mass is shared across |
| `count` | how many seeds funded the sighting |

An `anchor` sighting belongs to a recall seeded by its anchors alone: the fact was reached because it is filed under an anchor the query named. A `named` sighting belongs to a recall naming an anchor beside a term or a vector: the fact is filed under it, and the anchor raises it whether or not the fact matched anything itself.

<Note title="Contributions are not a sum" type="warning">

`score` on the hit remains the final value — after the fold and recency decay — so **the contributions explain its ingredients rather than summing to it.** The default fold measures each hit against the recall's whole pool, so the contributions explain a score exactly only when the recall returns every candidate, and a recall with a vector also needs the [vector weight](/docs/configuration/reference/database#vector-weight), which the payload does not carry.

</Note>

## Writes are rejected

A `remember` on this endpoint is rejected with 400: a write has no ranking to explain, and explaining must never mutate a graph.

## Reading a breakdown

A `text` or `vector` sighting is the fact's own match; together they are its seed mass $m$, the vector's similarity weighted by the vector weight. A `graph` sighting is an anchor the fact is filed under, carrying the mass $M_A$ the query's seeds put on it; it gives the fact only the part of that mass above what the anchor's degree predicts at the `background` rate $\rho_0$, with the fact's own mass excluded and shared across the anchor's edges. A `named` sighting owes no fair share, since the recall asked for that anchor:

$$
g = \sum_{\text{graph}} \frac{\max(0,\; M_A - m - d_A \, \rho_0)}{d_A} \;+\; \sum_{\text{named}} \frac{\max(0,\; M_N - m)}{d_N}
$$

A graph sighting at or below background adds nothing, so a hit can list anchors that did not move its score. A hit with only `graph` contributions matched nothing itself and was preempted in on transmitted surplus alone, which the model permits only when the surplus is above background; one with only `named` contributions matched nothing itself and is filed under an anchor the recall named.

Under the `information` fold, the default, a hit's channels are its text mass, its vector similarity above the lowest one among the recall's vector hits, and $g$; each is shared out over the recall's $n$ hits, and the hit scores $\sum_c w_c \log_2(1 + n\,p_c)$, where $p_c$ is its share of channel $c$ and $w_c$ the channel's share of the information the channels carry about the pool (see [The fold](/docs/retrieval/ranking/hybrid-retrieval#the-fold)). In the example, the two parrot facts put $1.5431 + 0.3461 = 1.8892$ on `polly`, so its three facts get $g = 0.1154$, $0.5144$ and $0.6297$: each the other two's mass over three edges, the third fact all of it. Over the four hits the text is concentrated on the first fact and the graph spread across the three `polly` facts, so the text carries $0.80$ bits about the pool and the graph $0.66$, weights $0.55$ and $0.45$, and the first fact scores $0.55 \log_2(1 + 4 \times 0.690) + 0.45 \log_2(1 + 4 \times 0.092) \approx 1.2517$.

Under the `excess` fold the hit scores $m + \alpha^2 g$ with $\alpha = 0.5$: graph evidence attenuated over the two edges and added to the fact's own mass. The fold and the lanes are introduced in [Hybrid retrieval](/docs/retrieval/ranking/hybrid-retrieval).
