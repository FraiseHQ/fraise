## Terms from the data model

- **fact** — a memory's atomic element: one self-contained statement, stored exactly as written, with the time it was written.
- **topic** — an anchor for what a fact is about, written `topic:billing`.
- **entity** — an anchor for who or what a fact mentions, written `entity:acme`.
- **anchor** — a topic or entity: a vertex facts are filed under, through which evidence is transmitted between facts.
- **memory graph** — a collection of facts linked to their topics and entities, indexed for hybrid search (full text, semantic and graph). An instance holds several, fully isolated from one another.
- **stream** — one query on its way through the system: the unit the engine plans, the scheduler queues and the database applies to one graph.

## Terms from the query language

- **command** — the verb a query starts with: `recall` or `remember`.
- **instruction** — a full query sent to the database engine: one command with its phrase or terms and its fields, on one line. Used interchangeably with *query*.
- **phrase** — a single-quoted literal: the fact in a `remember`, or a `recall` term a bare word cannot express, such as several words or a reserved word.
- **term** — a word or phrase a `recall` searches for; terms come before any field.
- **field** — a `name:value` clause: an anchor, a vector binding (`vec:`) or a modifier.
- **field value** — the part of a field after its `:`.
- **modifier** — a field that scopes a recall without seeding it: `since:`, `until:`, `depth:`, `top:`.
- **graph selector** — `@N` after the command, choosing which memory graph the query addresses; graph 0 when absent.

## Terms from the retrieval model

These are not part of the grammar, but they appear throughout the ranking documentation:

- **seed** — the initial evidence a query puts into the graph: a matched term, a named anchor, or a vector's nearest neighbours.
- **mass** — a seed's raw retrieval magnitude, in the units the source produced it in.
- **surplus** — the part of an anchor's observed mass that exceeds what its size alone would predict.
- **background rate ($\rho_0$)** — the query's seed mass per unit of anchor degree, over the anchors it reached. An anchor's degree times $\rho_0$ is its fair share: what it would hold if that mass were spread across those anchors in proportion to size.
- **lane** — which of the three `depth:` behaviours a recall takes: the BM25 floor, high precision, or maximum recall.

The model that uses them is in [Hybrid retrieval](/docs/retrieval/ranking/hybrid-retrieval) and [Excess transmission](/docs/retrieval/ranking/excess-transmission).
