---

- **fact** — a memory's atomic element.
- **memory graph** — a collection of organised facts related to topics and/or entities and indexed for hybrid search (full text, semantic and graph).
- **field** — additional information provided to the search in order to find the right facts.
- **field value** — the value of a field used to find a fact.
- **term** — a word or sequence of words used to find a fact.
- **command** — a task to perform by the engine: `recall`, `remember`.
- **instruction** — a full query sent to the database engine with a command, search terms and fields. Can be used interchangeably with *query*.

## Terms from the retrieval model

These are not part of the grammar, but they appear throughout the ranking documentation:

- **anchor** — a topic or entity node facts are filed under; the thing a graph walk moves through.
- **seed** — the initial evidence a query puts into the graph: a matched term, a named anchor, or a vector's nearest neighbours.
- **mass** — a seed's raw retrieval magnitude, in the units the source produced it in.
- **surplus** — the part of an anchor's observed mass that exceeds what its size alone would predict.
- **background rate (ρ₀)** — the mass a size-proportional smear of the query's seed mass across the graph's anchors would predict for an anchor of a given degree.
- **lane** — which of the three `depth:` behaviours a recall takes: the BM25 floor, high precision, or maximum recall.

The model that uses them is in [Hybrid retrieval](/docs/retrieval/ranking/hybrid-retrieval) and [Excess transmission](/docs/retrieval/ranking/excess-transmission).
