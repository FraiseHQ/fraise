# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

This project exists to build something useful together, and that only works if
everyone taking part is treated with respect.

We are committed to a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of
race, ethnicity, nationality, immigration status, skin colour, gender identity
or expression, sexual orientation, disability, neurotype, physical appearance,
body size, age, caste, religion (or lack of it), level of experience, or
socioeconomic status.

We want this to be a place where a first-time contributor asking a basic
question gets the same courtesy as a long-time maintainer.

## Expected behaviour

- Be kind. Assume the person you're replying to is acting in good faith.
- Be patient with people who are new, or working in a second language, or
  learning in public.
- Give feedback on the work, not the person. "This function has a race
  condition" — not "you clearly don't understand concurrency."
- Accept feedback gracefully. Being wrong in public is a normal part of building
  software.
- Respect people's stated names and pronouns.
- When you get something wrong, own it and move on. Apologies are cheap and
  repairs are valuable.

## Unacceptable behaviour

The following are not tolerated anywhere in this project:

- Racist, sexist, homophobic, transphobic, ableist, casteist, or otherwise
  discriminatory language, "jokes", or imagery — including as irony, sarcasm, or
  "just testing."
- Slurs, epithets, or dehumanising language of any kind.
- Personal attacks, insults, or sustained hostility.
- Harassment: unwelcome sexual attention, deliberate intimidation, stalking,
  following, or unwanted contact after being asked to stop.
- Publishing someone's private information — address, workplace, legal name,
  immigration status — without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately misgendering someone or persistently using a name they don't use.
- Dismissing or attacking requests for inclusion or accessibility.
- Encouraging or defending any of the above.

You do not have to agree with anyone's politics, identity, or worldview to work
alongside them here. You do have to treat them decently. This document governs
**conduct**, not belief — but conduct is not negotiable.

## Where this applies

This Code of Conduct applies across every space the project runs:

- **GitHub** — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and code
  comments.
- **[Discord](https://discord.gg/eHDFwnwHq)** — every channel, including voice, and
  including direct messages that begin from contact made in the server.
- **Project social accounts, talks, and events**, online or in person.

It also applies whenever you are representing the project in public.

**These spaces are one community.** Action taken in one applies to the others:
someone removed from Discord for harassment should not expect to carry on in the
repository as though nothing happened.

Serious harassment of a community member outside these spaces may also be
grounds for action here, particularly where it affects someone's ability to take
part safely.

## Moderators

Moderators are community members trusted to keep these spaces safe. They are
appointed by the maintainers and identified by role in Discord.

**What moderators do**

- Welcome newcomers and help people find the right channel or thread.
- Step into conversations that are turning hostile — redirecting, cooling down,
  or closing them.
- Remove messages, issue timeouts, and remove people from the server when this
  document is breached.
- Receive conduct reports and escalate them to the maintainers.

**How moderators work**

- **Safety first, process second.** A moderator can act immediately when
  something is serious or still happening, and explain afterwards.
- **Actions are not silent.** Moderators explain their reasoning to the person
  affected wherever it's safe to do so.
- **Moderators recuse themselves.** A moderator personally involved in an
  incident hands it to someone else rather than ruling on it.
- **Moderators are held to this document too** — in practice more strictly,
  because they set the tone. Reports about a moderator go to
  contact@getfraise.dev and are handled by the maintainers.
- **Moderation decisions are not debated in public channels.** Arguing with a
  moderator in-channel is itself disruptive. If you think a decision was wrong,
  use the appeals route below.

Being a moderator is a responsibility, not a rank. It does not come with extra
say over technical decisions, and it does not put anyone above this document.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness a problem, you have three routes. Use whichever
feels safest:

1. **Email contact@getfraise.dev** — reaches the maintainers.
2. **Message any moderator directly on Discord**. For anything happening in the
   server, this is usually fastest.
3. **Report to the platform** — [GitHub](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse)
   or Discord's in-app report. Use this if the problem is a maintainer, or if
   you'd rather not contact us at all.

You can report on someone else's behalf, and you don't need to be certain it
"counts", tell us what happened and we'll take it from there. Screenshots and
links help but are not required.

Reports are handled privately. We will not share your identity with the person
reported without your agreement, and we will not retaliate against anyone who
reports in good faith. Retaliating against a reporter is itself a serious
breach of this document.

You will get an acknowledgement within 3 days.

## Enforcement

Maintainers and moderators are responsible for enforcing this and will do so
consistently. Depending on severity and pattern, a response may be:

1. **A private word** — an explanation of what was wrong and what to do instead.
2. **A warning** — with conditions attached, such as no further interaction with
   the people involved.
3. **A temporary ban** from some or all project spaces.
4. **A permanent ban.**

Serious incidents, such as slurs, harassment, threats, doxxing, skip straight to the
end of that list. There is no strike system for those.

Maintainers and moderators may also edit, hide, or delete comments, commits,
code, issues, and Discord messages that violate this document, and will explain
the reason when they do.

### Appeals

If you've been warned, timed out, or banned and believe it was a mistake, email
contact@getfraise.dev within 30 days with your account of what happened. A
maintainer who was not involved in the original decision will review it.

We'll reply with a decision and a reason. Appeals are considered once per
incident.
